## assistant\n\nThis is a really common issue, and the good news is it's very fixable once you understand what's happening.

**The root cause is usually anticipation and preparation, not the legs themselves.** If you're late reading the ball, your feet freeze because your brain is still processing. By the time you decide to move, you're stuck hitting from a static, defensive position instead of stepping in.

Here's what to work on:

**Split step timing.** This is the single biggest unlock. You should be doing a small hop/landing right as your opponent makes contact. It loads your legs like springs and makes you feel explosive instead of flat-footed. Most club players either skip it entirely or do it too late.

**Unit turn before you move.** The moment you read forehand or backhand, turn your shoulders first. This naturally gets your weight shifting to the outside foot, which sets up the step-in. If you try to step in without the early turn, your body doesn't know where it's going.

**Think "catch the ball out in front."** Rather than cueing yourself to "step into the ball" (which can feel mechanical), try imagining you're catching the ball with your hand at the contact point out in front of your body. This naturally pulls you forward and into an athletic position.

**Load the back leg, push off.** On groundstrokes, your back leg should bend and load before you transfer forward. If your legs look shaky, you're probably standing too upright. Get lower — think about sitting into a chair slightly. The power comes from pushing up and forward off that back leg.

**A simple drill:** Have someone feed you mid-paced balls, and on every single shot, exaggerate stepping in with your front foot so aggressively that you end up a full step inside the baseline after contact. It'll feel weird at first, but it recalibrates your default positioning. You'll start finding that sweet spot where you're taking the ball on the rise with authority.

The shakiness and lack of confidence is almost always a symptom of being late and reactive rather than early and proactive. Fix the split step and early preparation, and the legs will follow.\n
