**Goblet squats** are the easiest entry point — hold a dumbbell or kettlebell at your chest. The weight in front naturally helps you stay upright and get good depth without falling backward.

**Key cues:**
- Feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed slightly out (10-30 degrees)
- Initiate by pushing your hips back *and* bending your knees together
- Keep your chest up — imagine someone's pulling a string from the top of your head
- Knees track over your toes (it's fine if they go past your toes, despite the old myth)
- Go as low as you can while keeping your heels down and back flat
- Drive through your whole foot on the way up, not just your toes

**If depth is hard:** Put small plates under your heels, or use a slightly elevated surface. Limited ankle mobility is usually the culprit — this gives you a workaround while you build flexibility.

**Box squats** are another good option — squat down to a bench or box, tap it (don't sit and relax), then stand back up. Takes the fear out of "how low do I go" and teaches you to control the descent.

Start with just bodyweight or very light weight. Get comfortable with the movement pattern before adding load. Given the "avoid injury above all else" philosophy from your longevity guide, this is exactly the right approach.\n

**Holding the weight:**
- Dumbbell: cup the top end with both hands, elbows pointing down
- Kettlebell: grip the horns (handles), bell facing up, or hold it upside down by the bell itself
- Keep it tight to your chest, not drifting forward — if it drifts, your lower back compensates

**The descent:**
- Think "sit between your legs" not "sit back" — this isn't a barbell back squat
- Your elbows should travel *inside* your knees at the bottom, not outside
- Control the speed down — 2-3 seconds. No dropping.
- At the bottom, your elbows can gently push your knees out for extra hip opening

**Core engagement:**
- Big breath into your belly before you descend, brace like someone's about to poke your stomach
- Keep that tension the whole way down and up
- Exhale only at the top

**Common mistakes to avoid:**
- Chest collapsing forward (keep it proud, shoulders back)
- Heels lifting (if this happens, ankle mobility is the issue — elevate heels or work on mobility)
- Knees caving inward (actively push them out, tracking over your pinky toes)
- Rising hips-first on the way up (chest and hips should rise together)

**A good self-check:** At the bottom position, you should be able to hold it comfortably for 5+ seconds. If you're wobbling or straining, something's off with the position. The goblet squat bottom is actually a great mobility position to just hang out in.

Start light — even 8-10kg is plenty while you're grooving the pattern.\n
