## assistant\n\nGreat question! Here's a concise comparison of H-1B advantages over the E-3 visa:

**Dual Intent** — H-1B explicitly allows immigrant intent, meaning you can pursue a green card without jeopardizing your status. E-3 is technically a non-immigrant visa, so demonstrating immigrant intent can be problematic (though in practice it's somewhat tolerated).

**Portability** — H-1B has well-established portability rules. You can start working for a new employer as soon as they file an H-1B transfer petition. E-3 portability is less clearly defined.

**Path to Green Card** — H-1B holders can stay in the U.S. indefinitely in 3-year increments while waiting for green card processing (via H-1B extensions beyond the 6-year limit under AC21). E-3 doesn't have an equivalent mechanism, so if your green card takes a long time, maintaining E-3 status gets trickier.

**Spouse Work Authorization** — H-4 spouses can get work authorization (H-4 EAD) if the H-1B holder has an approved I-140. E-3D dependent spouses currently cannot work in the U.S.

**No Country Restriction** — H-1B is available to nationals of any country, while E-3 is exclusively for Australian citizens.

**Broader Employer Familiarity** — Employers and immigration attorneys are far more experienced with H-1B processes, which can mean smoother filing and fewer complications.

**E-3 Advantages Worth Noting Though:**
The E-3 has its own perks — a separate annual cap (~10,500), renewability without limit on the number of 2-year extensions, simpler application process, and those unused E-3 slots roll into the general H-1B pool. So it's not strictly inferior.

The biggest practical difference is usually the dual intent / green card pathway issue. If you're planning to stay long-term and eventually get a green card, H-1B provides a much cleaner path. Want me to dig into any of these points further?\n
