# Knee Rehab and Asymmetry

## Current issue
The left side is clearly weaker and less stable than the right during split squats.

## Current baseline
- Left split squat: 15 lb dumbbells with noticeable wobble
- Right split squat: 20 lb dumbbells felt easy and stable

## Training interpretation
The left leg does not need more load yet. It needs more control, cleaner tracking, and consistent stability under moderate load.

## Progression rule
- Keep left-side load conservative until wobble is gone.
- Progress left only after clean reps across all working sets.
- Right side can be stronger, but do not let the gap grow unnecessarily.

## Split squat focus cues
- Front foot tripod stays planted
- Slight forward torso lean is good
- Front knee travels forward naturally over toes
- Knee tracks over 2nd/3rd toe
- 3-second lower, brief pause, smooth drive up
- Minimal push from back leg
- Every rep should look the same

## Practical plan
- Start with the left side every session
- Use 3 x 6–8 with a 3-second eccentric
- Give the left 1 extra set when useful
- Move to 20 lb on the left only after 3 clean sets of 8 at 15 lb with no wobble

## Pain rule
Some tendon loading discomfort is okay, but stop or regress if pain becomes sharp or instability clearly increases.
