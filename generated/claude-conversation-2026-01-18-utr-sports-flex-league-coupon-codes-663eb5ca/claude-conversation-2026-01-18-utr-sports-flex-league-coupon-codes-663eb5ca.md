Here's what you should know:

**How Oxidation Happens**

Oil oxidizes through exposure to heat, light, and air. The polyunsaturated fats in oils are most vulnerable because their double bonds react easily with oxygen. This process creates harmful compounds including aldehydes, peroxides, and free radicals.

**Signs of Oxidized Oil**
- Rancid or "off" smell (like crayons, paint, or cardboard)
- Bitter or unpleasant taste
- Darker color than fresh oil
- Sticky or gummy texture

**Health Concerns**

Consuming oxidized oils has been linked to increased inflammation, oxidative stress in cells, potential cardiovascular issues, and possibly accelerated aging. The aldehydes formed during oxidation are particularly concerning—they can damage cell membranes and DNA.

**Oils Most Prone to Oxidation**
- Flaxseed oil
- Walnut oil
- Fish oil
- Sunflower/safflower oil (high-linoleic versions)

**How to Prevent Oxidation**
- Store oils in dark, cool places
- Use opaque containers
- Keep lids tightly sealed
- Don't reuse frying oil multiple times
- Choose oils appropriate for your cooking temperature
- Pay attention to expiration dates

For high-heat cooking, more stable oils like avocado oil, refined coconut oil, or ghee are better choices since they resist oxidation at cooking temperatures.\n
