**Camera**

Position your camera at eye level or slightly above—never below, as it's unflattering. If you're using two cameras, your A-cam should be straight-on for the primary angle, and a B-cam can capture a tighter or side angle for visual variety in editing. Make sure your subject isn't too close to the background; pulling them forward creates depth and a nice separation. Natural light from a window (diffused with a curtain) works beautifully, or use a soft key light at about 45 degrees.

**Mics**

Audio matters more than video honestly. Lavaliers clipped about 6-8 inches below the chin work great for sit-down interviews—hide the cable under clothing. If you prefer a cleaner look, a shotgun mic on a boom just out of frame is excellent. Always do a sound check and listen for AC hum, fridge noise, or echo. Record in a room with soft surfaces (rugs, curtains, furniture) to dampen reverb.

**Laptop & OBS**

In OBS, set up separate scenes for each camera angle if you're switching live. Record at 1080p minimum, 30fps is fine for interviews. Use CBR encoding around 15-20 Mbps for good quality without massive files. Monitor your audio levels—aim for peaks around -6 to -12 dB. Have your laptop plugged in and close unnecessary apps to prevent dropped frames.

**Vibes**

Keep the background intentional but not distracting—bookshelves, plants, or simple textured walls work well. Offer your guest water. Keep the room slightly cool since lights warm things up. Play some low music before you start recording to ease tension, then cut it when you're ready.

**Mindset**

Do your research but don't over-script—have bullet points, not a rigid list. Listen more than you talk; your job is to draw them out, not perform. Embrace silence after they answer—people often fill it with something better. Start with an easy warm-up question to settle nerves. Be genuinely curious rather than just waiting for your next question.
