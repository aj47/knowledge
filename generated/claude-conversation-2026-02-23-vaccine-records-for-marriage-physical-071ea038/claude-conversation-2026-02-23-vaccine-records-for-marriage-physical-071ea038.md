## human\n\nFor the physical by civil surgeon for one of the forms for marriage I suspect I have some of the vaccines already. What should I do\n
## assistant\n\nBased on your context with visa/immigration processes, it sounds like you're preparing for the I-693 (Report of Medical Examination and Vaccination Record) that a civil surgeon completes as part of your green card application.

Here's what to do about vaccines you think you already have:

**Gather your existing records first.** Before your civil surgeon appointment, collect any vaccination records you have — childhood immunization records, records from your home country, any vaccines you've gotten from a doctor or pharmacy here. Even partial records help.

**Get a titer (blood) test.** If you don't have documentation but believe you were vaccinated, ask the civil surgeon to order titer tests. These blood tests check for antibodies and can prove immunity to diseases like MMR (measles, mumps, rubella), Hepatitis B, and Varicella (chickenpox). If titers show immunity, you won't need those shots again.

**Know what's required.** USCIS requires vaccines including MMR, Polio, Tetanus/Diphtheria, Hepatitis B, Varicella, Influenza (seasonal), COVID-19, and a few others depending on age. The civil surgeon will know the current list.

**Practical tips:**

- Call the civil surgeon's office before your appointment and ask what records to bring and whether they do titers in-office or send you to a lab — doing labs beforehand can save you a second visit.
- Some pharmacies (CVS, Walgreens) can pull up past vaccine records from state immunization registries, which might fill in gaps.
- If you end up needing vaccines, some civil surgeons charge a lot for administering them in-office. You can often get them cheaper at a pharmacy beforehand and bring proof.

This can save you from getting unnecessary duplicate shots and also reduce cost. Want help with anything else on the process?\n
## human\n\nCan I do it cheaper than civil surgeon\n
## assistant\n\nYes, definitely. Civil surgeons often mark up vaccines significantly. Here's how to save money:

**Get vaccines at a pharmacy or clinic first**

- **CVS, Walgreens, Costco pharmacy** — often the cheapest option, especially Costco (no membership needed for pharmacy)
- **Local health department** — sometimes offers vaccines free or at very low cost
- **Costco or Sam's Club pharmacies** tend to have the best cash prices

**The strategy:**

1. **Before your civil surgeon appointment**, check your state's immunization registry (you can call your state health department or ask a pharmacist to look you up) to see what's already on file
2. **Get titers drawn** at a lab like Quest or Labcorp — you may be able to order these yourself through services like **Walk-In Lab** or **Ulta Lab Tests** for $30-80 per panel, which is often cheaper than through the civil surgeon
3. **Get any missing vaccines** at a pharmacy with your insurance or at cash price
4. **Bring all documentation** (titer results + vaccine records) to the civil surgeon appointment

**The civil surgeon visit itself** is really just for the physical exam, form completion, and signing off. You're paying them to fill out the I-693 — no reason to also pay their markup on vaccines.

**Cost comparison example:** A civil surgeon might charge $200-400+ for vaccines on top of the exam fee, while the same shots at Costco pharmacy might run $50-100 total.

Just make sure all vaccines and titers are documented properly on paper that the civil surgeon will accept. Call their office ahead of time and ask exactly what format they need so nothing gets rejected.\n
