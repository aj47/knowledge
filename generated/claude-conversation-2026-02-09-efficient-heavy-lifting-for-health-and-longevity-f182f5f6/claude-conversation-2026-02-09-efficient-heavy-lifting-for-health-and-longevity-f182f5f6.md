Based on your longevity guide and the Attia/Johnson frameworks you're following, here's the most efficient approach to heavy lifting for health benefits:

**The big picture:** Three 45-60 minute full-body strength sessions per week, targeting all major muscle groups. Strength (not just muscle size) is what's independently protective against mortality.

**Most bang-for-buck movements:**

**Compound lifts** are where the efficiency lives — they hit multiple muscle groups simultaneously and have the strongest evidence for longevity markers like bone density, metabolic health, and functional strength:

- **Squat or leg press** — lower body + core, biggest bone density stimulus
- **Deadlift or hip hinge** — posterior chain, grip strength (grip strength is one of the strongest longevity biomarkers)
- **Overhead press** — shoulders, upper body pushing, shoulder stability
- **Row (barbell or cable)** — back, biceps, posture correction (especially important for you as a developer)
- **Loaded carry / farmer's walks** — grip, core, stability, real-world functional strength

**Efficiency tips given your setup:**

Since your apartment gym is 3 minutes away and you're already doing Zone 2 and tennis, keep strength sessions focused and time-efficient. A few principles from your guide worth emphasizing:

1. **Strength training first**, then low-intensity work — this is the order Johnson follows
2. **10 min stability work before lifting** — this is Attia's injury prevention pillar and aligns with the "avoid injury above all" philosophy
3. **Progressive overload** — the "heavy" part matters. You want to be working with weights that challenge you in the 4-8 rep range for strength, not just doing light sets of 15
4. **Don't skip grip work** — dead hangs, farmer's carries. Grip strength is one of the key metrics Attia tracks as a longevity predictor

The research from your guide is clear: consistency beats intensity, and one serious injury can derail years of progress. So go heavy enough to drive adaptation, but not so heavy you're grinding reps with bad form. You want to be a competitive athlete at 90, not burnt out at 35.
