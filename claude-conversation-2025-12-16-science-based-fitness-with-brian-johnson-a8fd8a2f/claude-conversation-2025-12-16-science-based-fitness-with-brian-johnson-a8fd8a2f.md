## The Key Stats That Matter Most

### VO2 Max (Cardiorespiratory Fitness)
This is arguably the most powerful longevity marker we have:
- A 2018 JAMA study of 122,007 adults found the lowest fitness group had nearly 4x higher mortality than the highest fitness group
- Going from low fitness (bottom 25%) to above average (50-75th percentile) is equivalent to eliminating end-stage renal disease as a risk factor
- Each 1 ml/kg/min increase in VO2 max was associated with a 45-day increase in longevity over 46 years of follow-up
- For every ml/kg/min increase from your personal baseline, you decrease your risk of all-cause mortality by about 10%

**Bryan Johnson's result**: VO2 max of 58.7 ml/kg/min, placing him in the top 1.5% of 18-year-olds

### Grip Strength / Muscle Strength
- A meta-analysis of 53,476 older individuals found the weakest quintile had 67% higher mortality than the strongest after adjustments
- Each 5kg decrease in grip strength was associated with a 16% higher risk of all-cause mortality and 21% increased risk of cardiovascular mortality
- Importantly, low muscle mass did not explain the strong association of strength with mortality—muscle quality matters more than quantity

## Bryan Johnson's Exercise Structure

His protocol combines strength training first, followed by low intensity exercise focusing on flexibility and mobility, with total sessions around 60 minutes

**Weekly targets**: Heart rate in zones 2-4 for 4.5 hours and zone 5 for 1.5 hours weekly

**The four pillars** (from Peter Attia's framework which aligns with Johnson's approach):
1. **Stability** – One hour per week, split into 5-10 minute blocks done before other workouts
2. **Strength** – Three 45-60 minute full body workouts targeting all major muscle groups
3. **Zone 2 (aerobic efficiency)** – Four 45-60 minute sessions
4. **Zone 5 (anaerobic/VO2 max)** – One to two 30-minute VO2 max sessions per week

---

## Zone 2: The Foundation

Zone 2 training is one of the four essential pillars for maintaining health and physical capabilities as we age—arguably the single most important tool for improving and sustaining mitochondrial health, which are central players in cellular aging and metabolic health

How to find it:
- Your heart beats at 70-80% of your maximum heart rate in Zone 2
- You can comfortably have a conversation for hours, but the person on the other end would notice you were exercising
