## assistant\n\nLooking at this digital advertising report, YouTube (via Google's Demand Generation campaigns) shows **mixed signals** as an investment opportunity:

## The Case FOR YouTube Investment:

**Competitive Cost Efficiency**: YouTube's cost-per-activation ($149-$163) is actually comparable to competitor search campaigns ($167), which is impressive given that YouTube targets cold audiences while search captures high-intent users.

**Proven Performance**: The campaigns are generating activations at scale - 52 activations from $7,808 spend in just 9 days (July 1-9).

## The Case AGAINST YouTube Investment:

**Scaling Challenges**: The report explicitly states YouTube is "scaling slower than expected." Despite having an $85,000 monthly budget, they've only spent $10,012 (just 12% of budget).

**Volume Limitations**: Even with reasonable cost-per-activation, the campaigns aren't spending their allocated budget, suggesting limited inventory or audience reach at current bid levels.

## The Strategic Question:

The team is considering two scaling options:
1. Increase bids beyond $200 cost-per-activation 
2. Launch view-optimized campaigns instead of activation-optimized

**Bottom Line**: YouTube isn't a "great" investment yet, but it's a **promising one that needs optimization**. The efficiency metrics suggest potential, but the scaling issues need to be resolved before calling it a clear win. The recommendation would be to test the proposed scaling strategies before significantly increasing investment.\n
