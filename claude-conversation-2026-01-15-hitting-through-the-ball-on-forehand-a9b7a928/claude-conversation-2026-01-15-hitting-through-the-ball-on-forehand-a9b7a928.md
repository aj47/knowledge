I'll give them practical advice without being too lengthy.That open face compensation is a classic symptom—your body's trying to create depth and clearance because the racket isn't moving forward through the ball enough.

A few things that might be happening:

**The swing path is going up too early.** If you're brushing up before you've actually driven through the contact zone, you lose forward penetration. Think "out, then up" rather than just "up." The racket should travel forward toward your target for a bit before the windshield-wiper finish kicks in.

**Tension in the arm or grip.** A tight grip kills racket head acceleration and makes it hard to stay through the ball. If you're squeezing, your arm decelerates at contact rather than whipping through. Try consciously relaxing the hand—some players think "loose fingers" right before the forward swing.

**Weight not transferring forward.** If your body stays back or you're hitting off your back foot, all you have is arm, and the natural compensation is to open the face to muscle the ball over. Loading into the shot with your legs and rotating through helps the racket travel forward naturally.

**A quick drill:** Set up a target (cone, ball can) about 3-4 feet in front of your contact point, roughly net height. Practice swinging *through* that target before finishing. It exaggerates the "hit through" feel and trains you to keep the face vertical longer.
